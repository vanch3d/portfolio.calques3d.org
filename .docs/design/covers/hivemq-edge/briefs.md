## Round 2 (2026-09-27): why these three candidates replace Round 1's

Round 1's three candidates used only points, straight/dashed lines, and thin arcs, almost always converging on or radiating from a single shared centre. On review this read as generic — the same underlying "N things resolve to one thing" template regardless of the actual project. See `.docs/tasks/2026-09-27-project-image-generation.md`'s "Round 2" section for the full diagnosis (the Vitruvian Man precedent, hatching-as-value, figural/asymmetric construction).

All three candidates below are rebuilt against a new required field — **Genericness check** — and use at least one technique Round 1 never touched: hatching/density-as-texture (Candidate 1), a measured before/after diptych with a genuine dimension line (Candidate 2), or a three-tier effort-weighted hierarchy instead of a flat ghost-fan (Candidate 3).

## Candidate 1 — "Twenty-Plus, One"

_(Round 1 called this "The Translation Layer" — same underlying fact, rebuilt composition.)_

**Source grounding:**

> "HiveMQ Edge translates twenty-plus industrial protocols, OPC-UA, Modbus, EtherNet/IP, decades-old equipment included, into MQTT, for a workforce that isn't made up of software developers." — `Portfolio_01_ProblemClass.md`, opening line

> "No screenshot needed here... a simple diagram works better than a screenshot: OT protocols on one side (OPC-UA, Modbus, EtherNet/IP, ageing PLCs), MQTT on the other, HiveMQ Edge as the translation layer between them. Keep it schematic, not a product screenshot." — same file, editorial note

**Concept statement:** The mark models heterogeneity resolved to uniformity — not three representative protocols standing in for "many," but the actual texture of "twenty-plus," genuinely dense and irregular, passing through a narrow aperture into one homogeneous band. The source's own number and its emphasis on age/vendor diversity are the specific facts; a token 3-element funnel would have been the same generic diagram Round 1 already got wrong.

**Composition:**

- Left two-thirds of the frame: a dense hatched field of roughly 20–24 short strokes, varying independently in length, angle, weight (mixing heavy/medium/ghost), and dash pattern (mixing solid/dashed), irregularly spaced — the actual heterogeneous mass of "twenty-plus industrial protocols, decades-old equipment included." No two strokes identical; the irregularity across four independent properties is the content, not decoration.
- A vertical frame line (heavy weight), roughly two-thirds across the canvas, with a single narrow gap (an aperture) at its vertical centre — the console's translation boundary. Strokes in the hatched field nearer the gap angle slightly toward it; strokes farther away stay scattered.
- Right third of the frame: a single uniform band of evenly spaced parallel strokes, identical weight and spacing (medium, regular) — MQTT as the resolved, standardised output.
- No accent colour (page-scoped One Red Rule — see Round 1 revision note below).

**Genericness check:** A hatched-mass-through-an-aperture-into-uniformity diagram could in principle serve any aggregation/normalisation concept — flagged **MARGINAL**, not a clean pass, and the candidate most worth stress-testing at Phase 4. What's meant to anchor it specifically to HiveMQ Edge is that the left field varies in _four_ independent stroke properties (length, angle, weight, dash) because the source names multiple simultaneous axes of difference (protocol type, age, vendor tooling) — a generic "many-to-one" diagram usually varies only one property (count, or length). If it still reads generic in the contact sheet, the fix is to push the irregular-vs-uniform contrast further (more strokes, tighter uniform spacing), not to abandon the underlying fact, which is genuinely HiveMQ-specific even if the diagram type is common.

**Rule variants:**

- Rule A (Construction Visible): add a faint ghost-weight guide line at the vertical centre, full frame height, marking where the boundary was placed, drawn before the frame/aperture line sits on top of it.
- Rule B (Resolved Only): no guide line — just the hatched field, the frame with its aperture, and the uniform band.

**Rule self-check:**

- One Red Rule: PASS by omission — page-scoped, this cover stays pure graphite.
- No-Decoration Rule: PASS — every stroke in the hatched field is load-bearing (the count and irregularity are the claim being made); removing strokes understates "twenty-plus."
- Compass Grammar: PASS — straight strokes, one frame line, one aperture gap; no rounding beyond what's compass-derivable.
- Flat-by-Construction: PASS — hatching is line-weight/density, never a gradient or shadow.

**Evolution note (planned, not yet executed — demonstrates the layered-enrichment process for this candidate specifically):**

- **Layer name / prospective `<g>` id:** `layer-operator`
- **Trigger:** proposed pre-emptively during Round 2 brief-writing, not yet triggered by an actual Phase 4 finding — recorded here as the planned next step _if_ this candidate is selected and judged to need more, per the evolution process in the parent task doc.
- **Source grounding for the new layer:** "The people who operate this equipment are experts in their domain and are not, as a rule, software developers. Their prior tooling experience is PLC configuration software, SCADA HMIs, vendor-specific panels..." — `Portfolio_01_ProblemClass.md`
- **Composition delta:** a small compass-constructed human silhouette (a circle for the head, a few arcs/lines for shoulders and one arm reaching toward the aperture) positioned just left of the frame line, roughly a third the scale of the hatched field — layered on top of the base construction as its own group, not replacing any existing element.
- **Rule self-check (new layer):** No-Decoration — PASS, directly grounded in a named, emphasised fact ("not software developers"). Compass Grammar — PASS, the figure is built from the same circle/arc primitives as the base, no new shape vocabulary introduced. Flat-by-Construction — PASS if the figure's form uses line-density (hatching) rather than any shading gradient. Genericness check — a bare human silhouette risks being generic on its own; paired with this specific hatched-field/aperture construction and reaching toward the specific console boundary, it becomes compositionally tied to this piece rather than a swappable stock icon — worth re-checking at the point this layer is actually drawn, not assumed to pass from this note alone.

## Candidate 2 — "Twenty-Seven Months"

_(Round 1 called this "The Bet That Worked" — same source, rebuilt around the chapter's own measured-interval framing rather than a static topology.)_

**Source grounding:**

> "I built a React Flow canvas instead, betting that a genuinely graph-shaped domain deserved a graph-shaped interface. Two years of continued investment after shipping it, rather than a rebuild, is the strongest evidence I have that the bet paid off." — `Portfolio_02_Workspace.md`, opening

> "For a 'then and now' pairing showing the scale of that growth, a retrospective Cypress screenshot taken against the codebase around PR #1308 (late November 2025) would capture Workspace at its most mature... set directly against the PR #51 screenshot above. Roughly twenty-seven months apart, same feature, same underlying premise." — same file

> "By late 2025, Workspace had grown from a single static graph into a system with ten node types, real-time status propagation across active and passive nodes, six layout algorithms, search and filtering, and a guided authoring flow, all still built on the same original premise." — same file, "The result"

**Concept statement:** The mark models _measured_ growth, not just "a graph" — a before/after diptych spanned by an actual dimension line — because the chapter itself frames its evidence as a measured interval between two concrete, dated artefacts (PR #51, PR #1308), not as a static topology sitting for a portrait.

**Composition:**

- Left panel (a light ghost-weight bounding frame, roughly square): a sparse graph — 3 nodes (small open squares), 2 edges at irregular, hand-placed angles, not grid-aligned — Workspace at PR #51, August 2023.
- Right panel (same-sized ghost-weight frame, positioned to the right with a visible gap between the two): a denser graph — 7 nodes, 6 edges, arranged with clear grid regularity — Workspace at PR #1308, November 2025: "ten node types... six layout algorithms... a guided authoring flow" compressed into visibly _more structure_, not just more nodes.
- Between the panels, at the bottom: a genuine dimension line — two short vertical witness lines dropping from each panel's outer edge, a heavy horizontal line between them with terminal arrows at both ends pointing inward. This is DESIGN.md's own named vocabulary ("dimension lines with terminal arrows") used directly, not left as an unused reference.
- No numeral on the dimension line — no text in the SVG per the i18n/portability rule; the measured-interval _device_ itself carries the meaning, not a printed "27 months" label.
- No accent colour.

**Genericness check:** A before/after diptych is a common device on its own — anchored here by three non-swappable facts: the panels' node/edge counts are the source's own documented counts at each PR (not round numbers chosen for symmetry), the left panel's hand-placed irregularity versus the right panel's grid regularity is Workspace's own documented arc (an organic feature growing into six formal layout algorithms), and the dimension line spans a real, cited interval (twenty-seven months) rather than a generic "time passed" gesture.

**Rule variants:**

- Rule A (Construction Visible): add the full implied construction grid (ghost dots) beneath the right panel only, extending slightly past its frame — showing the substrate the later, more-structured state was built on. The left panel stays ungridded in _both_ variants; the absence of a grid there is part of what "sparse, hand-placed" means, not something Rule A adds and Rule B removes.
- Rule B (Resolved Only): no grid dots anywhere — both panels read purely through node/edge density and placement regularity.

**Rule self-check:**

- One Red Rule: PASS by omission.
- No-Decoration Rule: PASS — panel node/edge counts are drawn from the source's own documented state at each PR; the dimension line is the chapter's own requested framing device ("pair it with a current-state screenshot for contrast"), not invented.
- Compass Grammar: PASS — sharp-cornered nodes and frames, straight edges, a genuine dimension-line construction with terminal arrows.
- Flat-by-Construction: PASS — density contrast (sparse vs. grid-regular) carries the growth; no shading.

## Candidate 3 — "Seven Attempts, One Current"

_(Same name as Round 1 — this candidate was already reasonably specific; deepened from a flat two-tier ghost-fan into a three-tier effort hierarchy.)_

**Source grounding:**

> "Workspace answers 'is this thing connected and is it running?' It deliberately doesn't answer a harder question... MQTT decouples publishers from subscribers so completely that some of the real relationships in this system don't exist as data at all, only as something computed at the moment you ask." — `Portfolio_04_DomainOntology.md`, opening

> "Seven visualisation attempts, presented as flip cards: Sankey, Chord, Sunburst, Cluster, Edge Bundling, Network Graph, Data Flow Tracer." — same file

> "The solution was to detect those specific relationships, call them feedback edges, render them as visually distinct dashed lines, and exclude them from Dagre's forward-layout pass entirely..." — same file, "Forcing a cyclic reality into an acyclic renderer"

**Concept statement:** The mark models _uneven_ effort across seven attempts, not seven identical tries. "Seven visualisation attempts" undersells what actually happened — some were explored briefly, one (the formal Domain DAG) developed much further before still needing the feedback-edge correction. Round 1's uniform ghost arcs erased that unevenness; a three-tier weighting restores it.

**Composition:**

- Seven arcs sharing one centre point, varying in _two_ properties: radius (as Round 1) and stroke weight/length. Three are short, very faint ghost strokes (small angular span, barely-there — quickly-abandoned paradigms). Three are longer ghost arcs with slightly more weight (partially explored). One is a distinctly heavier, longer arc, approaching medium weight — the attempt that got furthest (the formal Domain DAG) before still being superseded. This creates a genuine three-tier hierarchy (quickly-abandoned / seriously-explored / current) rather than Round 1's flat two-tier (ghost fan / current).
- One heavier graphite path emerging from the same origin — the actual current Domain DAG — containing one dashed segment (the feedback edge, lifted directly from the source's own illustration note), ending in a filled terminal dot.
- No accent colour.

**Genericness check:** "Several faint attempts around one current form" alone risks genericness — any iterative design process could use that shape. What's specific here is the _three-tier weighting_, directly reflecting the chapter's own account that one attempt (the formal DAG) got substantially further than the other six before still needing correction — a documented, specific unevenness, not a generic "trial and error" gesture.

**Rule variants:**

- Rule A (Construction Visible): the shared origin point marked with a small open compass-dot.
- Rule B (Resolved Only): no explicit origin mark — arcs and path emanate from an implied common origin.

**Rule self-check:**

- One Red Rule: PASS by omission.
- No-Decoration Rule: PASS — the weight variation directly encodes documented unevenness of effort, not arbitrary visual variety.
- Compass Grammar: PASS — arcs sharing a centre mark; the weight/length variation stays inside the existing stroke-weight vocabulary, no new primitive introduced.
- Flat-by-Construction: PASS — depth via a genuine four-step weight ramp (short-ghost / longer-ghost / heavier-but-superseded / current-heaviest) — the richest use of the tonal ramp of the three candidates.

## Round 1 revision note (2026-09-27, kept for reference): red accent removed — the rule is page-scoped, not SVG-scoped

DESIGN.md states the One Red Rule as "the compass-arc red appears exactly once **per surface**." In this project's own vocabulary, "surface" means the whole page/route — `.impeccable/surfaces/` keys its direction-contract files by page component path (`src-app-projects-slug-page-tsx.md` for this exact route), not by component. The `/projects/[slug]` page this cover renders into already spends its one red mark on `ClassificationHeader`'s status stamp ("status stamp (ONGOING red / else graphite)" per `.docs/tasks/2026-09-08-project-surface.md`). All three candidates above stay pure graphite for this reason — the ongoing/archived signal stays exactly once, at the status stamp, not duplicated into the cover.
