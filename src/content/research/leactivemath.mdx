LeActiveMath was a European FP6 project building a web-based adaptive learning environment for
secondary and university-level mathematics, with natural language interaction and personalised
content delivery. My contribution, across positions at Northumbria, Glasgow, and Edinburgh
universities, was to design and integrate an Open Learner Modelling (OLM) component into the
broader system.

Open Learner Modelling — making the system's internal student model inspectable and challengeable
by the student — was at the time an active research frontier in AIED. The question was whether
showing students what the system "thought" about their competencies would improve their engagement,
motivation, and reflection.

My approach was to specify an Extended Open Learner Model (xOLM), a multi-layered evidence-based
model drawing on Dempster-Shafer Theory (DST), capable of representing mathematical competency at
multiple levels alongside affective and motivational factors, meta-cognition, and misconceptions.
I then designed the OLM visualisation component — the interface through which students could explore
the system's judgements, inspect justifications, and challenge them — and implemented it in Java
(Java Servlets, JSP, Java Applet, Swing/SWT) for integration with the main LeActiveMath system.

The work produced several FP6 deliverables and led to a journal article in the *International
Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education* (2007) on opening up the interpretation process
in open learner models.
