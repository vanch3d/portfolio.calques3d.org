Calques 3D is a three-dimensional Dynamic Geometry microworld, designed to help secondary-school
students visualise and explore spatial geometry through direct manipulation of geometric objects.
The system was the central artefact of my PhD research at LORIA, Université Henri Poincaré Nancy I,
and continued to evolve — through user-driven updates and international collaborations — until 2010.

The research problem at the heart of Calques 3D was not purely technical: it was about how to
design Interactive Learning Environments (ILEs) that are genuinely adapted to teachers' needs.
My approach was to propose a participatory design process in which teachers were involved at every
stage of the production cycle, from requirements gathering through iterative prototyping to
classroom evaluation. This produced both a usable software tool and a replicable methodology for
co-design of educational software with domain experts.

Implemented in C++ with MFC, GDI/GDI+, and OpenGL, Calques 3D provided a direct manipulation
interface for 3D geometric objects — points, lines, planes, polyhedra — with real-time visual
feedback on geometric relationships. A key design constraint was that the interface had to conform
to pedagogical constraints negotiated with the working group of secondary teachers, not merely
technical affordances.

From 2006 to 2010, the project entered a second life through international scientific collaborations.
Work with Prof Humberto Bortolossi (Universidade Federal Fluminense, Brazil) produced user-driven
feature extensions. A separate collaboration with Prof Eugenio Roanes-Lozano (Universidad Complutense
de Madrid) and Dr Francisco Botana (Universidade de Vigo) resulted in a communication interface
between Calques 3D and the computer algebra system Maple, enabling automatic theorem proving within
3D geometric constructions — a contribution later published in *Mathematics and Computers in
Simulation* (2010). Calques 3D is now abandonware.
