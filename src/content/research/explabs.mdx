The Digital Health Institute (DHI) is a Scottish Funding Council innovation centre led by the
University of Edinburgh and the Glasgow School of Art. At the core of the DHI programme are the
"Experience Labs" — a person-centred, design-driven research approach providing opportunities
for extreme collaboration between academics, business, and civic partners to co-create sustainable
solutions with end-users.

During my time at GSA, I led or contributed to several Experience Lab projects, each using a
different design method with participants placed in real-life or simulated scenarios:

**Directory App** — Mockup wireframing for the co-design of a mobile-based service directory
for paramedics. Methods: rapid prototyping with POP 2.0, roleplaying.

**Digital Brokering** — Experimenting with a mapping tool, based on Actor-Network Theory, for
the elicitation of community-based networks of needs, services, and training opportunities.
Methods: focus groups, ANT mapping.

**Talking Medicines** — Scoped and designed a co-design session for an Augmented Reality
medical packaging mobile app, with real-life formative field evaluation. The project was
cancelled before execution.

**Backpack** — Roleplaying and co-design of a boardgame for investigating the potential of
Personal Data Stores for MS citizens. Published as a research proposal with the DHI.

Across all labs, the focus was on pre-technology knowledge elicitation — understanding what
participants actually needed, how they understood their context, and what was worth building —
before any lines of code were written.
