This early research project, conducted as part of my DEA (Master's equivalent) at LORIA,
Université Henri Poincaré Nancy I, investigated whether Inductive Logic Programming (ILP) could
be used to automatically construct models of student misconceptions in planar geometry.

The motivating problem was diagnostic in an Intelligent Tutoring System: when a student
constructs a geometric figure incorrectly, generating a relevant explanation requires understanding
_why_ their reasoning was wrong, not just that it was. The hypothesis was that student productions,
however incorrect, are internally consistent with the student's implicit model of the domain. By
applying induction over the domain theory and the student's examples, it should be possible to
construct an alternative theory that _explains_ those productions — i.e. a model of the student's
misconceptions.

Because the geometry ITS used First-Order Logic as its knowledge representation, ILP offered
natural possibilities: I adapted existing ILP systems (black-box paradigm) to the task and
evaluated the quality of the induced models. While results were encouraging, the research
identified a key limitation: the absence of counter-examples (cases where the student's
reasoning _doesn't_ apply) severely constrains the induction process. More interactive
elicitation of such examples would be required to make the approach robust.
