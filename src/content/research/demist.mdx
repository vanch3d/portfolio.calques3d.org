DEMIST (Design Environment for Multi-representational Instructional Simulation Technology) was
a simulation-based learning environment developed as a research platform at the ESRC Centre
for Research in Development, Instruction and Training (CREDIT), University of Nottingham,
under Dr Shaaron Ainsworth.

The research question driving DEMIST was fundamental to learning science at the time: does
learning with multiple external representations (graphs, tables, animations) actually help
students, and if so, under what conditions? Empirical results were mixed, and the field lacked
both a coherent theoretical framework and a controlled experimental platform capable of testing
it.

DEMIST was designed to fill the latter gap. Built in Visual C++ with MFC and GDI+, it implemented
a simulation model of population dynamics that could be configured to show any combination of
representations simultaneously, with fine-grained authoring control over the number, sequence,
and degree of translation support between representations. This flexibility made it possible to
test the DeFT framework (Design, Functions, Tasks) — developed with Dr Ainsworth — in
controlled lab experiments with students.

The system included a multi-faceted authoring interface that allowed researchers to configure
simulation parameters, representation combinations, and interaction constraints without code
changes. Several formative and summative evaluations were designed and run with student
participants, examining both learning outcomes and the cognitive load imposed by different
representational combinations.
