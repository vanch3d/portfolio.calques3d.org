This project was a collaboration between the Learning Sciences Research Institute (LSRI) and
the NIHR National Biomedical Research Unit in Hearing (NBRUH) at the University of Nottingham,
under Profs Mike Sharples and Deborah Hall. The central research question was whether auditory
perceptual learning could be delivered through engaging casual games, suitable for home-based
use without clinical supervision.

My approach was to reframe auditory training as a life-long and life-wide intervention by
establishing a clear distinction between auditory *testing* (assessment) and auditory *training*
(therapeutic engagement). This distinction informed a three-part work plan: designing multiple
casual games for training on specific auditory tasks; adapting user-centred adaptive methods
appropriate for this context; and deploying the games within an intervention-based platform.

I designed two casual games for Auditory Discrimination Therapy (ADT) — repurposing familiar
game mechanics (Bubble, Simon) for purely auditory interaction — implemented in Flash/AS2 and
JavaScript/HTML5. These were tested against a traditional computer-based ADT system in a
randomised controlled clinical study with 60+ tinnitus patients in a 6-week home-training
intervention.

The study collected engagement data (flow experience, return-to-task behaviour) alongside
audiological outcomes (tinnitus improvement). Results were published in *PLoS ONE* (2014),
demonstrating gameplay as a source of intrinsic motivation in auditory training.
